# GridCast — Suggested 3–4 Minute Presentation Script

**Slide 1 — Title**  
Good morning. Our mini-project is GridCast, an electricity-consumption analytics and demand-forecasting system. It uses data-mining and time-series techniques to convert historical observations into understandable patterns and short-term estimates.

**Slide 2 — Problem and objective**  
High-frequency readings are not easy to interpret directly. We prepare a daily time series, identify the main trend and possible weekly pattern, compare forecasting methods, and present their measured errors in a dashboard.

**Slide 3 — Dataset**  
For the final experiment, we use the UCI Individual Household Electric Power Consumption dataset. It contains more than two million minute-level observations from one household in France from December 2006 to November 2010. The dataset contains missing readings, and it is important to acknowledge that the data is historical and represents one household rather than a whole electricity grid.

**Slide 4 — Workflow**  
The workflow is extract, transform, explore, decompose, forecast, evaluate and present. We preserve chronological order so the test period occurs after the training period; we do not randomly shuffle the time series.

**Slide 5 — Methods**  
We use descriptive analytics and a seven-day moving average. Weekly decomposition helps inspect trend, seasonal and residual components. For forecasting, we compare a moving-average baseline, a seasonal-naive baseline, linear trend and ARIMA(2,1,2).

**Slide 6 — Dashboard**  
The dashboard includes a daily trend, moving average, monthly and weekday comparisons, decomposition, model evaluation and future predictions. The visual shown here is generated from bundled synthetic sample data for demonstration. The final report should show plots generated from the actual UCI dataset.

**Slide 7 — Evaluation**  
We compare forecasts against the final chronological holdout window. MAE shows average absolute error, while RMSE penalizes larger errors more strongly. The model with the lowest holdout RMSE is best only for that chosen dataset and test period. We will report the actual values generated by the real-data run.

**Slide 8 — Limitations and future work**  
Our limitations include the historical single-household dataset, estimated daily energy and interpolation of missing days. Future work could add weather and holiday variables, test seasonal ARIMA, use rolling-origin evaluation and include multiple meters.

**Slide 9 — Conclusion**  
GridCast demonstrates a complete time-series analytics workflow: cleaning, exploratory analysis, forecasting, evaluation and interactive presentation. Thank you. I am ready for questions.
